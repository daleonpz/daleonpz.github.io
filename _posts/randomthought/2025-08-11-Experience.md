I’ve heard a lot of people say things like “experience is the best teacher” or “some things you can only learn through experience.”

But... what is experience, really?

At the end of the day, experience is just knowledge. It’s something you gain over time — but time alone doesn’t guarantee that you've actually learned anything valuable.

Just because someone has “years of experience” doesn’t necessarily mean they’re knowledgeable. You could be doing the same thing wrong for years, or just repeating the same task over and over without actually growing or improving.

Now... Reading or studying can help speed up that knowledge-building process — but of course, it depends on what you’re reading and it's quality. Sadly, clear and well-explained information is hard to find.

Sure, you can't learn to ride a bike just by reading about it. But reading up on how to ride one — what to expect, what to avoid, where to focus — will help you get the hang of it faster. You’ll have a mental model to guide you.

Reading isn't a magical solution. It gives you a model of how things work — and models are never perfect. They simplify reality, leave out details, or miss certain angles entirely.

To get knowledge and a deep understanding of things, you need hands-on sessions — like in engineering and science. They're there to test and refine the theory through real-world application.

When you experiment, you're basically saying: “Here’s what I expect based on what I know—let’s see how it holds up.” If things don’t go as expected, you get to ask: Was the theory flawed? Or did I misunderstand something? That’s how real learning happens.

So personally, I think we should look at experience as how knowledge was gained—not just time spent doing something. Time and experience are related, but they’re definitely not the same thing.
